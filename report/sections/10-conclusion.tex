\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The central objective of this research was to determine whether a parameter-efficient fine-tuned language model trained on anonymized mobile network logs can accurately forecast a subscriber's next cell attachment. Over nine weeks of experimental investigation across eleven iterative configurations, the empirical results confirm that fine-tuned language models achieve superior prediction accuracy compared to strong persistence baselines, with optimal performance governed by specific structural user conditioning mechanisms.

\section{Summary of Key Findings}

Three primary empirical findings summarize the contributions of this research, as presented in \textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:results-card}}:

\input{figures/fig-results-card}

\paragraph{Demonstrated Baseline Outperformance via Behavioral Conditioning.} A fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B language model achieves a \textbf{+4.3 percentage point accuracy gain} over the persistence baseline on active mobility evaluation cohorts (54.5\% vs. 50.2\% top-1 accuracy, as highlighted in Card 1 of \textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:results-card}}). Surpassing the persistence baseline required user behavioral stratification ($G0$--$G3$) and per-group LoRA adapter routing, which increased model accuracy from -1.8 percentage points behind the baseline to +3.7 percentage points ahead on baseline active mobility splits.

\paragraph{Data Cohort Scaling Saturation at 2,000 Subscribers.} Scaling training cohort size yields significant accuracy improvements up to 2,000 subscribers (+4.2 percentage points over baseline, as depicted in Card 2 of \textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:results-card}}). Expanding training cohorts further up to 21,000 subscribers yields no statistically significant improvement (+0.04 percentage points, $p > 0.05$), establishing 2,000 subscribers as the empirical operating threshold for training set construction.

\paragraph{Dominance of Subscriber Typology over Data Volume.} Individual subscriber mobility characteristics represent the primary determinant of trajectory predictability. Prediction accuracy across behavioral user groups spans a \textbf{22.4 percentage point range} (62.8\% for stationary $G0$ vs. 40.4\% for mobile $G3$, as shown in Card 3 of \textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:results-card}}), outweighing the impact of dataset scaling beyond 2,000 subscribers ($0.4$ percentage points) by a factor of over fifty.

\section{Experimental Limitations and Unresolved Tasks}

Three unresolved technical validation steps are identified for future verification:
1. \textbf{Cross-Cohort Model Rescoring}: Rescoring earlier experimental checkpoints across the unified 1,000 held-out evaluation cohort to place all iteration metrics on a single standardized baseline axis.
2. \textbf{Multi-Seed Parameter Variance Estimation}: Evaluating multi-seed training runs to quantify variance across stochastic optimization initializations.
3. \textbf{LoRA Adapter Capacity Control}: Training a single LoRA adapter with equivalent overall parameter capacity to isolate the contribution of adapter capacity expansion versus group routing.

In addition, hardware constraints limited scaling sweeps to a fixed gradient step budget (8,000 steps), leaving full multi-epoch training on 21,000 subscribers for future evaluation.

\section{Perspectives and Future Work}

\paragraph{Short-Prefix User Stratification.} While per-group adapter routing yields significant accuracy gains, identifying user group assignment ($G0$--$G3$) currently relies on observing extended historical trajectory prefixes. Developing short-prefix classification algorithms would allow early-stage trajectory routing for incoming subscribers.

\paragraph{Multi-Day Trajectory Personalization.} Expanding trajectory sequence windows beyond single 24-hour periods to multi-day trajectories would enable capturing weekly subscriber routine patterns and individual personalization.

\paragraph{Cross-City Generalization.} Evaluating model transferability across denser urban wireless networks with higher base station densities represents a key direction to test spatial generalization.

\section{Executive Summary}

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of language models on single-day mobile network logs achieves superior next-cell forecasting accuracy over persistence baselines (+4.3 percentage points top-1 accuracy gain in \textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:results-card}}). Training dataset scaling saturates at approximately 2,000 subscribers, beyond which individual subscriber mobility typology exerts the dominant influence on predictability (22.4 percentage point spread). Stratifying subscribers by temporal mobility profiles and routing input sequences to specialized LoRA adapters represents the primary architectural driver of trajectory forecasting performance.
