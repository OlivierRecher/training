This report asks whether a large language model,\footnote{\textbf{LLM}:
\emph{large language model}. A neural network trained to predict the next symbol
of a sequence. Nothing in that definition is specific to language, which is why
one can be pointed at a trajectory; see Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}.} fine-tuned on
anonymised 2G/3G\footnote{\textbf{2G}, \textbf{3G}: the second and third generations of mobile radio.
Both appear in this corpus, and which one served a handset is readable from
the cell identifier. \textbf{Fine-tuning}: changing a model's internal
numbers with task-specific data, rather than leaving them frozen and only
rewriting its instructions.} mobile-network traces, can
predict a user's next \cid{cell\_id}\footnote{\cid{cell\_id}: the identifier
of one radio cell, e.g.\ \cid{BSOCHO1}. It is a structured string, not an opaque
token: see Figure~\ref{fig:cellid-anatomy}.} better than the rule ``repeat the
last cell seen''. That rule, the \emph{persistence
baseline}, is the yardstick for every claim made here, and it is a hard one: on
a raw day of records it is correct 69.2\,\% of the time, and for five of the
eleven weekly iterations of this internship nothing I trained beat it.

The work was carried out on fifteen daily exports covering the Karlovy Vary
study area, 369 radio cells over 113 physical base stations, with one day of
records per user. Three results came out of it. First, a fine-tuned
Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA\footnote{\textbf{LoRA}: \emph{Low-Rank Adaptation}, and \textbf{QLoRA} its 4-bit
variant: fine-tuning methods that freeze the original model and train a small
set of extra numbers, an \emph{adapter}, beside it. Figure~\ref{fig:lora}
draws the mechanism.} adapters clears the baseline by
\textbf{+4.3 points}\footnote{\textbf{Point} means \emph{percentage point} throughout: 43.1\,\% to 46.8\,\%
is $+3.7$ points, not $+3.7$ percent. Every result here is a difference of two
accuracies.} of top-1
accuracy,\footnote{\textbf{Top-1 accuracy}: the share of predictions whose highest-ranked
candidate is the correct \cid{cell\_id}. \textbf{Top-3} and \textbf{top-5} ask
whether it is among the three or five highest-ranked: the same predictions
read at three depths.} reproducibly and on two independent held-out test sets, and
what made the difference was neither model capacity nor data volume but
conditioning on \emph{who the user is}: grouping users by the hours at which
their physical site actually changes, and routing each group to its own weights.
Second, the value of population size is exhausted early: the edge grows from
$+0.6$ to $+4.2$ points between 400 and 1{,}900 training users, and a further
ten-fold increase to 21{,}000 users buys $+0.1$ point, with zero inside every paired-bootstrap confidence
interval.\footnote{\textbf{Paired bootstrap}: a significance test that resamples the
\emph{same} test users for both models compared, so the luck of the draw
cancels. An interval containing zero means the two cannot be told apart.
Detailed in Chapter~\ref{ch:tools}.} One mechanism fits the timing, the
smallest of the four behavioural profiles crosses roughly a hundred members
exactly there. Third, and most robustly, difficulty lives in the user rather than
in the dataset: 22 points separate the most sedentary behavioural profile from
the most mobile one, against three tenths of a point across the entire
training-size ladder.

A secondary finding concerns measurement rather than modelling. The three largest
single improvements of the internship were reading the best checkpoint instead of
the last ($+15.7$ points), recognising that the context cut belongs to the
measurement rather than to the model, and choosing a corpus on which the trivial
rule does not already score 69\,\%. On a task whose honest yardstick is a
one-line heuristic, the measurement deserves to be audited before the
architecture is.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} human mobility prediction, call detail
records,\footnote{\textbf{CDR}: \emph{call detail record}. The row an operator
writes whenever a handset and the network interact: a pseudonymous identifier, a
timestamp, and the serving cell. No content of any kind.} next-cell prediction,
large language models, LoRA, QLoRA, behavioural clustering, scaling.
