\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

This report asks whether a large language model, fine-tuned on anonymised 2G/3G
mobile-network traces, can predict a user's next \cid{cell\_id} better than the
rule ``repeat the last cell seen''. That rule, the \emph{persistence baseline},
is the yardstick for every claim made here, and it is a hard one: on a raw day
of records it is correct 69.2\,\% of the time, and for five of the eleven
iterations of this internship nothing I trained beat it.

The work was carried out on fifteen daily exports covering the Karlovy Vary
study area, 369 radio cells over 113 physical base stations, with one day of
records per user. Three results came out of it. First, a fine-tuned
Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA adapters clears the baseline by \textbf{+4.3 points} of
top-1 accuracy, 54.5\,\% against 50.2\,\% on the 1{,}000 held-out users of the
largest corpus, and the same configuration measures $+4.2$ points on a second,
independent test set. What made the difference was neither model capacity nor
data volume but conditioning on \emph{who the user is}: grouping users by the
hours at which their physical site actually changes, and routing each group to
its own weights. Second, the value of population size is exhausted early: the
edge grows from $+0.6$ to $+4.2$ points between 400 and 1{,}900 training users,
and a further ten-fold increase to 21{,}000 users buys $+0.1$ point, with zero
inside every paired-bootstrap confidence interval. One mechanism fits the
timing, the smallest of the four behavioural profiles crosses roughly a hundred
members exactly there. Third, and most robustly, difficulty lives in the user
rather than in the dataset: 22.4 points separate the most sedentary behavioural
profile from the most mobile one, against four tenths of a point across the
entire training-size ladder.

A secondary finding concerns measurement rather than modelling. The three
largest single improvements of the internship were reading the best checkpoint
instead of the last ($+15.5$ points), recognising that the context cut belongs
to the measurement rather than to the model, and choosing a corpus on which the
trivial rule does not already score 69\,\%. On a task whose honest yardstick is
a one-line heuristic, the measurement deserves to be audited before the
architecture is.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} human mobility prediction, call detail
records,\footnote{\textbf{CDR}: \emph{call detail record}. The row an operator
writes whenever a handset and the network interact: a pseudonymous identifier, a
timestamp, and the serving cell. No content of any kind.} next-cell prediction,
large language models, LoRA, QLoRA, behavioural clustering, scaling.
