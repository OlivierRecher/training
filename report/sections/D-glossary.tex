\chapter{Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

The terms that recur from chapter to chapter, each in one or two sentences.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\small
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelindent=0pt,labelsep=0.4em,
                    itemsep=2.5pt,parsep=0pt]

\item[Base station, cell, \cid{cell\_id}.] A \emph{base station} is one physical
  installation. It serves three or four \emph{cells} pointing in different
  directions, in each radio generation, and each cell is what the working files
  call a \cid{cell\_id}, for instance \cid{UKVCHE102}, whose middle letters
  identify the base station. Mobility is always measured on the base station,
  because 12.4 out of every 100 back-to-back records change cell without
  changing place; what has to be predicted stays the full \cid{cell\_id}.

\item[Cell-switch rate.] Out of every hundred back-to-back records of one user,
  how many land on a different cell than the one before. Near 0\,\% for
  somebody who stays home all day, near 100\,\% for somebody constantly moving.
  It is the single property that best predicts how well that user is scored.

\item[Correct answers per hundred.] How every result here is expressed. The model
  gaining 4.3 means it answers correctly 54.5 times out of a hundred where the
  persistence baseline answers correctly 50.2 times, on the same users and moments.

\item[Epoch.] One complete pass of the model over all the training data. A run
  lasts several epochs, and the copy kept is the one from the best epoch.

\item[Held-out users.] Users set aside before training and never shown to the
  model, on which every reported score is measured.

\item[Hour symbol.] A symbol \cid{<H00>} to \cid{<H23>} placed before each event.
  Always handed to the model, never predicted by it, because the current time is
  known when a prediction is asked for.

\item[Kind of user, or behavioural profile.] One of four groups of users, sorted
  automatically by how often their \emph{base station} changes over four broad stretches
  of the day, named by increasing mobility: G0 the stay-at-home user, G1 the
  steady user, G2 the evening-moving user, G3 the morning-moving user.

\item[LoRA, QLoRA.] Training methods that freeze the model's original numbers and
  train a small set of new ones beside them, about 2 in every 100 here. QLoRA
  additionally stores the frozen numbers in a coarser form.

\item[Paired resampling.] A test in which the same test users are redrawn at
  random for both models being compared, so the luck of the draw cancels out. A
  range containing zero means the two cannot be told apart.

\item[Persistence baseline, or one-line rule.] The rule ``the next cell is the
  last one seen''. No model, no training, and not a constant: 69.2\,\% on a raw
  day and 43.1\,\% on users who genuinely move, because the users differ.

\item[Protocol: last fifth, or whole day.] ``Last 20\,\%'' grades only the final
  fifth of a test user's day; ``whole day'' grades every position of it, which
  is harder and lowers the persistence baseline on the very same users from 45.5\,\% to
  43.1\,\%.

\item[Record, or event.] One \cid{cell\_id} paired with one time. Not a movement:
  an untouched phone still produces records, because the network asks it to
  announce itself on a fixed cycle.

\item[Share of the day given, or context fraction.] How much of a user's day is
  handed to the model before it has to produce the rest, 80\,\% by default. It
  belongs to the measurement rather than to the model: moving it moves the
  persistence baseline too. Not the model's \emph{context window}, the hard limit of 512
  symbols it may look at in one go.

\item[Top-1, top-3, top-5.] Whether the cell the user really visited is the
  model's first guess, or anywhere among its first three or first five out of the
  369 it knows.

\item[User.] One line of a day file: an anonymized mobile line, identified by
  its \cid{user\_id} for that day, and the sequence of cells it attached to.

\end{description}
\end{multicols}
