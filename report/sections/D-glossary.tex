\chapter{Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

The terms that recur from chapter to chapter, each in one or two sentences.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\small
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelindent=0pt,labelsep=0.4em,
                    itemsep=2.5pt,parsep=0pt]

\item[Antenna, mast, \cid{cell\_id}.] A \emph{mast} is one physical installation.
  It carries three or four \emph{antennas} pointing in different directions, and
  each of those is what the working files call a \cid{cell\_id}, for instance
  \cid{UKVCHE102}, whose middle letters identify the mast. Mobility is always
  measured on the mast, because 12.4 out of every 100 back-to-back records change
  antenna without changing place; what has to be predicted stays the full antenna
  name.

\item[Cell-switch rate.] Out of every hundred back-to-back records of one person,
  how many land on a different antenna than the one before. Near 0\,\% for
  somebody who stays home all day, near 100\,\% for somebody constantly moving.
  It is the single property that best predicts how well that person is scored.

\item[Correct answers per hundred.] How every result here is expressed. The model
  gaining 4.3 means it answers correctly 54.5 times out of a hundred where the
  one-line rule answers correctly 50.2 times, on the same people and moments.

\item[Held-out people.] People set aside before training and never shown to the
  model, on which every reported score is measured.

\item[Hour symbol.] A symbol \cid{<H00>} to \cid{<H23>} placed before each event.
  Always handed to the model, never predicted by it, because the current time is
  known when a prediction is asked for.

\item[Kind of user, or behavioural profile.] One of four groups of people, sorted
  automatically by how often their \emph{mast} changes over four broad stretches
  of the day, named by increasing mobility: G0 the stay-at-home user, G1 the
  steady user, G2 the evening-moving user, G3 the morning-moving user.

\item[LoRA, QLoRA.] Training methods that freeze the model's original numbers and
  train a small set of new ones beside them, about 2 in every 100 here. QLoRA
  additionally stores the frozen numbers in a coarser form.

\item[One-line rule, or persistence baseline.] The rule ``the next antenna is the
  last one seen''. No model, no training, and not a constant: 69.2\,\% on a raw
  day and 43.1\,\% on people who genuinely move, because the people differ.

\item[Paired resampling.] A test in which the same test people are redrawn at
  random for both models being compared, so the luck of the draw cancels out. A
  range containing zero means the two cannot be told apart.

\item[Protocol: last fifth, or whole day.] ``Last 20\,\%'' grades only the final
  fifth of a test person's day; ``whole day'' grades every position of it, which
  is harder and lowers the one-line rule on the very same people from 45.5\,\% to
  43.1\,\%.

\item[Record, or event.] One antenna name paired with one time. Not a movement:
  an untouched phone still produces records, because the network asks it to
  announce itself on a fixed cycle.

\item[Share of the day given, or context fraction.] How much of a person's day is
  handed to the model before it has to produce the rest, 80\,\% by default. It
  belongs to the measurement rather than to the model: moving it moves the
  one-line rule too. Not the model's \emph{context window}, the hard limit of 512
  symbols it may look at in one go.

\item[Top-1, top-3, top-5.] Whether the antenna the person really visited is the
  model's first guess, or anywhere among its first three or first five out of the
  369 it knows.

\end{description}
\end{multicols}
