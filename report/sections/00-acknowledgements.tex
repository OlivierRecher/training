My first thanks go to \supervisor, who supervised this internship and took the
time, week after week, to explain the parts of the mobile-network side of the
problem that no amount of reading would have given me: what a signalling record
actually is, how cells and base stations relate to one another, and why a
question that looks like plain sequence prediction is constrained by the way the
network itself is built. That patience shaped the direction of the work, and most
of the corrections that mattered came out of those conversations.

I am equally grateful to the small group of interns working on the same corpus,
Nino, Tiphain, Benjamin and Reda, for their help throughout these two months.
They were already several months into their own analyses when I arrived, and they
explained the corpus, the conventions and the pitfalls to me rather than leaving
me to rediscover them. Nino in particular went out of his way, repeatedly, to
produce the extracts I needed with the tooling he had built for his own analysis,
which spared me a great deal of work and made most of the experiments in this
report possible at all. Tiphain and Benjamin discussed their move-or-stay framing
and their prompting results openly, and two of the ideas I reused here are
theirs. Working next to them made those two months both easier and considerably
more interesting than they would otherwise have been.
