\figtext{%
\figpara{What was varied.}{Only one thing: where each person's day is cut into
the part the model reads and the part it has to produce. ``70 / 30'' means the
model sees the first 70\,\% of the day and is graded on the last 30\,\%.
Everything else is frozen.}

\figpara{The columns.}{\textbf{Valid.} is the score on people held back during
training, used only to decide when to stop. \textbf{Model} is the score on the
test people. \textbf{Rule} is what ``repeat the last antenna'' scores on those
very same moments. \textbf{Distance} is the model minus the rule, and it is the
only column that means anything. Top-3 and top-5 read the same predictions less
strictly.}

\figpara{The point of the table.}{The \textbf{Rule} column changes from row to
row, and that is the whole lesson. Moving the cut changes which antenna counts as
``the last one seen'', so it changes the competitor too. Each row must therefore
be read against the rule printed on its own line, never against the model score
on the line above.}

\figpara{The upper block, and the mistake it contains.}{On \sfilt, exactly one
cut out of three clears the rule, by 1.1. The other two lose ground, by 0.3 and
by 2.4. One favourable cell in a table of three is not a result, and the next
iteration made it disappear.}

\figpara{The lower block.}{On \smob, no cut clears the rule at all. The model's
score climbs steadily from 41.5 to 47.6\,\% as it is given more history, which
looks like progress until one notices that the rule climbs just as fast on the
same rows. Qwen2.5-0.5B with QLoRA, 100 held-out people.}}
