\figtext{%
\figpara{Where these hours come from.}{They are not chosen. For each kind of
user, I look at the score of the one-line rule hour by hour \emph{on that kind
alone}, and keep the run of consecutive hours where it falls furthest below that
same kind's own average. In plain terms: the hours at which ``repeat the last
antenna'' stops working \emph{for that sort of person}. The multiplier says how
much more a mistake costs there.}

\figpara{The first row is the one a hand-made rule could never produce.}{The
stay-at-home user gets no emphasised hours at all, because their day is flat and
there is nothing to emphasise. Leaving every hour at its ordinary cost is the
correct answer for 28\,\% of the population, and a single window chosen for
everybody structurally cannot say that.}

\figpara{The last two rows are almost disjoint in time.}{The evening-moving user
is emphasised late, the morning-moving user early. That is the mirror-image fact
of Figure~\ref{fig:group-profiles} restated in the language of the training
procedure, and it is why one shared set of hours was doomed.}

\figpara{The lower block is the test that justifies the whole detour.}{An
emphasised stretch of the day is only useful if it actually lands on hard
moments. The guess of Chapter~\ref{ch:time} separates the rule by two tenths,
which is to say not at all: the moments inside it are exactly as easy as the
moments outside. The hours derived per kind of user separate it by 27.4, marking
moments on which the trivial rule is right 19\,\% of the time against 46\,\%
elsewhere. Whatever the model then makes of that emphasis, it is at least being
aimed at the right places.}}

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}\footnotesize
The guess I started from was not merely imprecise, it was misplaced. 04--06h is
\emph{easier} than average for all four kinds of user: the one-line rule scores
between 49 and 64\,\% inside it, against 20 to 57\,\% on average. Emphasising it
therefore spent the training effort on moments the rule already gets right, which
is the most expensive mistake available on a task like this one. It survives in
the code only as the fallback used when the sorting is switched off or the data
carries no hours.
\end{minipage}
