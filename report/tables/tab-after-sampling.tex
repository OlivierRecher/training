\figtext{%
\figpara{What is compared.}{The same 100 held-out people and the same grading,
before and after the three changes described in the text. Each row is one cut:
``30\,\%'' means the model was given the first 30\,\% of each person's day as
history. These are the moments \emph{outside} the two emphasised stretches of the
day, which is to say the great majority of them.}

\figpara{The columns.}{\textbf{Distance before} and \textbf{Distance after} are
the model minus the one-line rule, in correct answers per hundred, on that row's
moments. A negative number means the model is behind the rule.
\textbf{Change} is how much that distance moved, and every value in it is
negative. The last two columns read the same predictions less strictly.}

\figpara{Why seven rows and not one.}{A single degraded measurement would be
noise. Seven of them, at seven different cuts, all moving the same way, is a
model that has been narrowed: it got better at the hours it was told to care
about and worse everywhere else. Top-3 and top-5 fall by between 2.6 and 4.8
across the board, so this is not a matter of the right answer slipping from first
to second place. It has left the shortlist entirely. \smob, 100 held-out
people.}}
