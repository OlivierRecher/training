\caption{What a pair of back-to-back records actually is}
\label{fig:transition-composition}
\figtext{%
\figpara{What is being counted.}{Take every pair of back-to-back records in a
file and ask what separates the two. Each bar is 100\,\% of one file's pairs,
cut three ways. Dark blue: the same antenna name, so nothing at all happened.
Teal: the antenna name changed but the mast did not, so the phone hopped to
another antenna of the same installation while the person stayed where they
were. Orange: the mast changed too, so the person genuinely went somewhere.}

\figpara{The middle band is the trap, and filtering cannot remove it.}{On an
ordinary day only 44.0 pairs out of a hundred are a real movement, and 12.4 are
the middle band: they look like movement in the raw data and are not. Once only
mobile people are kept, real movement rises from 44 to 51, which is the whole
point of that filtering, while the middle band stays at 12. That band is a
property of the radio network, not of the people. Counting it as mobility would
inflate every movement figure here by about a quarter and blur the four kinds of
user of Chapter~\ref{ch:groups}, so mobility is always measured on the mast. The
prediction target deliberately stays the full antenna name, and the price of
that is accepted openly rather than hidden (Chapter~\ref{ch:user}).}}
