\caption{Composition of consecutive-event pairs}
\label{fig:transition-composition}
\figtext{%
What a pair of back-to-back records actually is. Each bar is 100\,\% of one
corpus's consecutive-event pairs, split three ways: dark blue where the two
records carry the same \cid{cell\_id}, teal where the \cid{cell\_id} changed
but the site root did not, so the handset re-attached to another antenna of
the same mast, and orange where the site root changed too. On \sraw{},
43.6\,\% of pairs repeat the same \cid{cell\_id} outright and a further
12.4\,\% change cell without changing place; only 44.0\,\% are a real site
change. Restricting the corpus to mobile, non-absent users (\smob{}) moves the
mass towards real movement, 51\,\%, while the middle band stays at 12\,\%,
which says that band is a property of the radio network rather than of the
population. Counting it as mobility would inflate every movement statistic by
roughly a quarter and would scramble the behavioural profiles of
Chapter~\ref{ch:groups}, so every mobility measurement in this report is taken
on the site root. The prediction target deliberately remains the raw
\cid{cell\_id}, and the consequence, that the model is marked wrong for a
difference that is not a movement, is accepted rather than hidden
(Chapter~\ref{ch:user}).}
