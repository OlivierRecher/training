\caption{The three things a pair of back-to-back records can be}

\figtext{%
\figpara{Figure~\ref{fig:transition-composition}: transition types and event composition.}{Take every pair of back-to-back records in a file
and ask what separates the two. Each bar in Figure~\ref{fig:transition-composition} is 100\,\% of one file's pairs, cut three
ways. Dark blue: the same antenna name, so nothing at all happened. Teal: the
antenna name changed but the mast did not, so the phone hopped to another antenna
of the same installation while the person stayed where they were. Orange: the mast
changed too, so the person genuinely went somewhere.}

\figpara{Figure~\ref{fig:transition-composition}: sector handover breakdown.}{On an
ordinary day in Figure~\ref{fig:transition-composition}, only 44.0 pairs out of a hundred are a real movement, and 12.4 are
the middle band: they look like movement in the raw data and are not. Once only
mobile people are kept, real movement rises from 44 to 51, while the middle band
stays at 12. That band is a property of the radio network, not of the people, so
mobility is always measured on the mast. The prediction target deliberately stays
the full antenna name, and the price of that is accepted openly
(\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:user}}).}}
