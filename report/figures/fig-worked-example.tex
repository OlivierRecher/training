\caption{What one prediction is}
\label{fig:worked-example}
\figtext{%
\figpara{The two kinds of moment, and why only one of them counts.}{The rows are
two moments of the same person's day. Column A is the past the model may read,
each moment written as an hour and an antenna name; column B is the moment I want
answered, hour given and antenna hidden; column C compares the rule's answer and
my model's against what really happened. In row 1 the person has not moved, so
``the same as before'' is correct and the rule scores a point without knowing
anything. Moments like that are the majority, which is why a rule with no model
in it is right about seven times out of ten. In row 2 the person has left, and
the rule now has to be wrong, because being wrong is all it can do when somebody
moves. That is the only kind of moment on which a model can gain anything, and
every gain in this document was won there.}}
