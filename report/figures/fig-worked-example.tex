\caption{One prediction, taken apart}
\label{fig:worked-example}
\figtext{%
\figpara{The two kinds of moment, and why only one of them counts.}{The rows are
two moments of the same person's day, and the letters stand for two antennas. On
the left is the past the model may read, each moment an hour and an antenna; in
the middle is the moment I want answered, hour given and antenna hidden; on the
right, the rule's answer and my model's against what really happened. In the first
row the person has not moved, so ``the same as before'' is correct and the rule
scores a point without knowing anything. In the second the person has left, and
the rule now has to be wrong, because being wrong is all it can do when somebody
moves. That is the only kind of moment on which a model can gain anything.}}
