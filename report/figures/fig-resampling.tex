\figpara{\panelref{fig:resampling}{a}: trajectory length equalization accuracy comparison.}{In \panelref{fig:resampling}{a}, every
person's day is stretched to exactly 200 records by repeating records already
there, so that the longest quarter of people stops carrying 44\,\% of the training
material. Each run is shown against \emph{its own} one-line rule, and only the
comparison inside a pair means anything. The levelled model reaches 74.9\,\%, by a
wide margin the highest absolute score of the project, but the rule on those same
stretched days reaches 76.5\,\%.}

\figpara{\panelref{fig:resampling}{b}: breakdown of pure copy moments versus real decision moments.}{In \panelref{fig:resampling}{b}, stretching a day
fills it with copies, and a copy is a moment on which repeating the last antenna
cannot be wrong. That is why both scores rose by about thirty in \panelref{fig:resampling}{a}. On the 1{,}738
moments in \panelref{fig:resampling}{b} that are real decisions, the model is behind. Its bar there is derived from
the reported 74.9\,\% assuming it never misses a copy, and it stays below the rule
at either end of that assumption. And because a copied step never changes
location, levelling compresses the spread between people by a factor of 2.3,
destroying the very signal the four kinds of user are cut on.}}
