\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering

{\textbf{One instruction from the other branch,} exactly as it is sent to a
model whose numbers are never modified. Quoted from Tiphain and Benjamin's work
and shortened at the two places marked \texttt{[...]}; nothing else is
altered.\par}
\vspace{3pt}

\begin{lstlisting}
User's day started at 00:00 at station VSOYRO. [...] They stayed at BSOROT
until 05:34, then left for station VOYQZ, arriving at 06:15. [...] It is now
07:45 and the user is at VSOYRO.
Predict the station the user will visit next. Rely only on their movement
pattern and the stations around them. Possible stations include: BKVOR4,
BKVOF2, BKVS2D, BSOROT, BSVOLOK, DRVOAN, DSXVOP, LKVTSE. Which station are
they most likely to go to next?
Look at the user's daily flow (loops, pauses, commuting, travel habits).
Explain your choice in a few natural sentences, then end with the station in
this format:
FINAL ANSWER: [STATION]
\end{lstlisting}

\caption{The other branch: one instruction sent to an untouched model}
\label{fig:prompting}
\figtext{%
\figpara{The same problem, asked the other way round.}{Compare this page with
Figure~\ref{fig:training-example} and the difference between the two branches of
the team becomes concrete. Here the day is narrated in English, stop by stop and
with clock times, so a single moment costs a whole clause where my model reads
two symbols. A person with a long day stops fitting in the model's window long
before a short one does.}

\figpara{The candidate answers are handed over in the instruction.}{Eight
antennas are listed for the model to choose from, and the answer comes back as
free text that then has to be parsed, which is what the \texttt{FINAL ANSWER}
convention is for. My model instead picks among the 369 antennas it already
carries in its vocabulary.}

\figpara{And nothing is remembered from one person to the next.}{Since no number
inside the model ever moves, everything the model is meant to know about this
population has to be restated in every single instruction. The whole point of
the branch this report takes is the opposite: what the population has in common
ends up stored in the numbers.}

\figpara{Neither is the better instrument.}{Asking a general model what it
already knows about a day described in words, and asking what can be learned
from the population itself, are two different questions. The second one is
this report's.}}

\end{figure}
