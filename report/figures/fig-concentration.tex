\figtext{%
\figpara{Interpretation of the metric bars in Figure~\ref{fig:concentration}.}{One row per candidate property of a person, named on
the left in plain words in Figure~\ref{fig:concentration}. The bar is the Pearson correlation of
Equation~\eqref{eq:pearson} between that property and that person's own score:
zero means the property tells you nothing about the score, $\pm1$ that it tells
you everything, and pointing left means ``more of this, worse predicted''. Dark
blue is measured on the 1{,}173 scored people of a raw day file, teal on the 100
held-out people of a different set.}

\figpara{Correlation ranking and top-bar warning of Figure~\ref{fig:concentration}.}{How often
somebody changes antenna reaches $-0.971$ on the first set and $-0.773$ on the
second in Figure~\ref{fig:concentration}; how spread out their time is ($-0.811$) and how much sits on one main
antenna ($+0.696$) are the same information from other angles. Everything else,
including how much history the person has, stays below $0.24$. But $-0.971$ is
close to arithmetic, since how often somebody changes antenna is the mirror image
of how often the one-line rule is right on them, so any model imitating that rule
earns a large negative value before anything is measured.}}
