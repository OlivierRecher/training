\figtext{%
\figpara{Figure~\ref{fig:group-gain}: cohort-stratified accuracy gains.}{In Figure~\ref{fig:group-gain}, there is one pair of bars per kind
of user, plus the plain average of the four on the right. Grey-blue is the
one-line rule, dark blue fine-tuned model, both on exactly the same moments of the same
100 held-out people, over every position of their day. The gain ranks in the
precise reverse order of the rule, with no exception among the four. The
stay-at-home user, whom repetition already predicts 56.0\,\% of the time, gains
0.8; the morning-moving user, whom repetition predicts less than one time in five,
gains 6.4; the steady user gains 2.3 and the evening-moving user 5.4.}

\figpara{Figure~\ref{fig:group-gain}: reporting conventions and group distribution caveats.}{In Figure~\ref{fig:group-gain}, the 3.7 is the plain
average over four kinds counted once each; weighted by how many people each kind
holds it is 3.3, and that is the figure an operator would experience, because
28\,\% of the population sits in the kind that needed no model at all. And the
mechanism depends on the kind of user being identifiable, which
\textbf{\panelref{fig:group-difficulty}{b}} shows collapsing below roughly 60\,\% of a
day of history.}}
