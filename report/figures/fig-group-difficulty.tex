\caption{The kind of user predicts difficulty, and can be read from the past}

\figtext{%
\figpara{Panel (a): the label says in advance how hard somebody will be.}{Two
bars per kind of user, on two different scales, so read each colour against its
own neighbours. Orange is how often that kind changes location over the day;
grey-blue is what the one-line rule scores on them. The two rankings are exact
opposites, with no exception: the stay-at-home user changes location about a
third of the time and the rule is already right 56.3\,\% of the time for them,
while the morning-moving user changes half again as often and the rule is right
only 17.8\,\%. That is what makes the label useful before any model runs.}

\figpara{Panel (b): the constraint that makes the idea usable, and where it
breaks.}{At the moment a prediction is asked for, only the person's past exists,
so the kind of user has to be worked out from that past alone or the exercise is
circular. These bars measure how often the kind identified from the past matches
the kind computed from the complete day. Given 80\,\% of the day it is right for
92 of the 100 held-out people, unaided. Below roughly 60\,\% it collapses,
61\,\% at half a day and 44\,\% at 30\,\%, because there is not enough history to
tell a morning mover from an evening mover. That is an uncomfortable place for
the limit to fall: it is worth nothing on somebody who has just appeared, which
is exactly when an operator would most want a prediction. \smob, 400 training
and 100 held-out people.}}
