\begin{figure}[t]

{\footnotesize\textbf{(a) One user-day, as it is stored on disk.} One line of
the training file \texttt{400\_users\_train.jsonl}, reformatted here over
several lines for legibility. Nothing is a conversation in any real sense: the
three roles are simply the slots the training library expects.\par}
\vspace{3pt}

\begin{lstlisting}
{"conversations": [
  {"role": "system",    "content": "You are an AI that generates a user's
                                    cell-visit trajectory."},
  {"role": "user",      "content": "Here is the sequence for this user."},
  {"role": "assistant", "content": "User 2832452 | 200 events |
                                    BSOCHO1 BSOCHO1 UKVCHE102 BSOCHO1 ..."}
]}
\end{lstlisting}

\vspace{2pt}
{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8cm}p{11.15cm}@{}}
\texttt{system} & The task description. Identical on every line of the file. \\
\texttt{user}   & The request. Also identical everywhere: it is a placeholder,
                  not a question. \\
\texttt{assistant} & The answer the model must learn to produce, and the only
                  part its mistakes are counted on. \\
\end{tabular}\par}

\vspace{0.55cm}
\hrule height 0.4pt
\vspace{0.45cm}

{\footnotesize\textbf{(b) The same line, once the tokenizer has read it.} Each
\cid{cell\_id} is one symbol, not a spelling; each event is preceded by its
hour. Only the shaded positions contribute to the loss.\par}

\caption{What one training example looks like}
\label{fig:training-example}
\figtext{%
``The conversational fine-tuning format of TRL'' means only this. (a)~Each
user-day is written as a three-slot JSON record. The \texttt{system} and
\texttt{user} slots are boilerplate, repeated identically on every line of the
file, and the whole trajectory lives in the \texttt{assistant} slot; choosing
that format buys nothing conceptually, it buys compatibility with a library
that already knows how to read it. (b)~Reading the same line left to right is
what the model actually sees: the prefix sets the scene and is masked out of
the loss, each hour token is handed to the model for free because the current
time is always known when a prediction is requested, and only the
\cid{cell\_id} positions are ever scored. A trajectory of 200 events costs 400
tokens plus a short prefix, which is why the hour is coded as one symbol
rather than written out as a time.}
\end{figure}
