\caption{Where the day is cut, and how each moment is answered}
\label{fig:protocol}
\figtext{%
\figpara{Panel (a): the cut.}{Each test person's day is split once into a part
the model is allowed to read and a part it has to produce. The default split is
80/20, meaning the model sees the first four fifths of the day and is graded on
the last fifth. Chapter~\ref{ch:first} shows that where this cut falls is part of
the \emph{measurement} rather than a property of the model, which is why it is
drawn here rather than in the chapter about tools.}

\figpara{Panel (b): one moment at a time.}{The model is not asked to invent a
whole afternoon. It is asked for one antenna, then told what really happened,
then asked for the next one. This matters for two reasons. It cannot see the
future, and it cannot compound its own errors, so a single early mistake does not
poison everything after it.}

\figpara{And the competitor answers the same questions.}{The one-line rule is
run through exactly the same moments, with exactly the same history available to
it, and simply answers the last antenna it was shown. That is what makes the two
numbers comparable at all.}}
