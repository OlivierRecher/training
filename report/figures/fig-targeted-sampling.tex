\caption{Emphasising two stretches of the day: the win, and the price}
\label{fig:targeted-sampling}
\figtext{%
\figpara{What was changed before this run.}{Training slices containing a move
inside one of the two chosen stretches of the day were shown three times more
often than the rest, and a mistake at 18--20h was made to cost four times an
ordinary one instead of twice. Nothing else moved.}

\figpara{How to read the four pairs.}{Each pair is one group of graded moments,
with the number of moments printed under its label so the reader can see how much
weight it carries. Grey-blue is the one-line rule, dark blue is my model, both on
exactly the same moments. The number above each pair is the distance between
them.}

\figpara{The win, and it is a real one.}{At 18--20h the model gains 2.6 correct
answers per hundred, and over the two chosen stretches combined 2.3. This is the
first time in the whole project that a trained model beat ``repeat the last
antenna'' on a segment chosen \emph{in advance} rather than found afterwards, and
that distinction is what makes it worth reporting at all.}

\figpara{The price, and it is larger.}{The fourth pair is everything outside those
stretches: 7{,}893 moments, more than ten times the volume of the win, and the
model loses 2.6 there. So the overall result of this run is clearly negative.
Making some mistakes count more buys correct answers where the weight was put and
pays for them everywhere else, which is exactly what the mechanism is designed to
do.}

\figpara{Which leaves one question, and it decides the next chapter.}{The trade
is only worth making if the hours that were emphasised really are the hard ones.
Table~\ref{tab:group-windows} shows that they were not. \smob, 100 held-out
people, every position of the day graded. The 04--06h bar rests on 128 moments
only and should be read as ``not worse'' rather than as a win.}}
