\caption{Targeted transition sampling}
\label{fig:targeted-sampling}
\figtext{%
The first time in the project that the model beat ``repeat the last cell''
anywhere, and the price. Four groups of scored positions from one run on the
same 100 held-out users, with the number of predictions printed under each
label; the persistence baseline is in grey-blue and the model in dark blue,
and the gap is annotated above each pair. Oversampling training windows that
contain a transition-window event, and weighting $18$--$20$h four times
instead of twice, buys $+2.6\pt$ at $18$--$20$h and $+2.3\pt$ over the two
windows combined. It costs $-2.6\pt$ on the 7{,}893 predictions falling
outside them, more than ten times the in-window volume, so the aggregate is
clearly negative. Weighting a chosen set of positions buys accuracy there and
spends it elsewhere, which is what it is designed to do; whether the trade is
worth making depends on the emphasised hours being the hard ones, and
Table~\ref{tab:group-windows} shows they were not. \smob{}, 100 held-out
users, full sequence. The $04$--$06$h result rests on 128 predictions and
should be read as ``not worse'' rather than as a win.}
