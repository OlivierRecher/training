\caption{The score on unseen people, pass by pass, on four sets of people}
\label{fig:validation-curves}
\figtext{%
\figpara{What is plotted.}{Four separate training runs on four different sets of
people. The horizontal axis counts the passes a run made over its training data;
the vertical axis is the score on people it is \emph{not} training on. The solid
line is the model, the star marks its best pass, and the dashed line is the
one-line rule on that same set of people. In all four the score climbs for four
to nine passes, peaks, then declines, while the score on the run's own training
data keeps improving: that divergence is the model switching from learning how
the population moves to learning its examples by heart.}

\figpara{What it costs to read the wrong pass.}{In panel (a) the best pass scores
65.0\,\% and the last one 48.1\,\%, so reading the end of the run instead of its
best point throws away 16.9 correct answers per hundred plus forty passes of
waiting time. Measured on the held-out people rather than the validation ones the
same choice costs 15.5, and it is the single largest improvement of the
internship.}

\figpara{Careful with the dashed line.}{It moves between panels, from 69.2\,\%
on the unfiltered day to 43.5\,\% once only mobile people are kept, because the
people are different. The four panels are comparable only on the distance between
solid and dashed, and in all four that distance is negative: none of these runs
beats the rule. The per-pass points are read off each run's own chart and carry
about one correct answer per hundred of reading error; the figures quoted in the
text are the reported ones.}}
