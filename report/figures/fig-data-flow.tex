\caption{From a raw file to training material, in four steps}
\label{fig:data-flow}
\figtext{%
\figpara{The raw line, at the top.}{This is one person's whole day as it arrives
from the operator. The first eight fields are administrative; everything after
them alternates the name of an antenna and the time, in seconds since midnight,
at which the phone was seen on it.}

\figpara{The four steps.}{\textbf{(1)} Throw away everything that is not a
trajectory and keep the anonymous number, the record count and the
antenna-and-time pairs. \textbf{(2)} Turn each time into the hour of day it
falls in, so that 23{,}822 seconds becomes ``hour 6''. \textbf{(3)} Write one
line per person: their day as an ordered list of events. \textbf{(4)} Split the
people into those the model studies and those it is graded on.}

\figpara{The two circles.}{They hold different people, never the same ones, and
the split is balanced so that both sides get the same mix of short and long
histories. The 400 and 100 shown here are the figures for the mobile-user day;
the other rows of Table~\ref{tab:datasets} go through the same four steps with
different counts.}}
