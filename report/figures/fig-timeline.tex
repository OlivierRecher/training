             text width=11.0cm,align=left},

\draw[clrule,thick] (0,0.4) -- (0,-21.2);
\foreach \i/\date/\body in {
  0/{3--8 Jul}/{\textbf{1 --- Documentation, and a first signal.} Which fine-tuning regime fits a free cloud GPU? Chose QLoRA over full fine-tuning and plain LoRA, built the cleaning and formatting pipeline. \textbf{16.87\,\%} next-cell accuracy on a Mistral-7B run that saw 1.2\,\% of the data.},
  1/{9--14 Jul}/{\textbf{2 --- The baseline appears, and the model is under it.} Abandoned Mistral-7B (5.5\,h, best validation 41.1\,\%) for Qwen2.5-0.5B. Engineering-only fixes lifted test top-1 \textbf{48.7\,\% $\rightarrow$ 64.4\,\%} and cut GPU time by 60\,\%, but the persistence baseline of that data sits at \textbf{69.2\,\%}.},
  2/{14--17 Jul}/{\textbf{3 --- Who is hard, and why.} Per-user analysis on the held-out users: accuracy correlates with the cell-switch rate at \textbf{$r=-0.97$}, and 10\,\% of users carry 59\,\% of the evaluation signal. The model's value-add lives in the segment that barely counts.},
  3/{17--21 Jul}/{\textbf{4 --- Filter the corpus, and win once.} Removed users with too few records or a 4-hour hole in their activity period. \textbf{80.1\,\% vs a 79.0\,\% baseline: $+1.1\pt$} --- the first time the model cleared it, and only at one context split out of three.},
  4/{21--24 Jul}/{\textbf{5 --- A cleaner corpus, and the win evaporates.} Moved to Nino's mobile, non-absent users. Swept the context fraction from 30\,\% to 90\,\%: \emph{no} split beats the baseline (best $-0.3\pt$). Per-user diagnosis: some users collapse to 0\,\% precisely at the end of the day.},
  5/{27--29 Jul}/{\textbf{6 --- Give the model the clock.} Added one hour token per event and a heavier loss inside 04--06h / 18--20h. The hour \emph{is} read (shuffling it costs $-3.0\pt$). Targeted sampling then won the windows (\textbf{$+2.6\pt$} at 18--20h) and lost everywhere else ($-2.6\pt$).},
  6/{29 Jul -- 2 Aug}/{\textbf{7 --- Group users by behaviour.} Four profiles cut on the hourly \emph{site}-change rate, detected from the context prefix alone (92\,\% agreement). The old global window was shown to separate nothing ($+0.2$ pp) while per-group windows separate $-27.4$ pp. First cross-training run: \textbf{inconclusive} --- ablations showed the model ignored both the group and the hour.},
  7/{2--7 Aug}/{\textbf{8 --- Make the group unignorable.} Four changes; the structural one routes each group to its own adapter instead of prepending a token. \textbf{46.8\,\% vs 43.1\,\%: $+3.7\pt$} --- the first run of the project to clear the baseline. Gains rank inversely to group difficulty ($r=-0.99$).},
  8/{12--17 Aug}/{\textbf{9 --- More users.} 1{,}000 / 1{,}500 / 2{,}000 training users on a new day-file: \textbf{$+2.8$, $+3.5$, $+4.2\pt$}. Also tested levelling every user to 200 records: 74.9\,\% looks spectacular against a baseline that reaches 76.5\,\% on the same data --- the gap changes sign.},
  9/{18--19 Aug}/{\textbf{10 --- Much more users.} 5{,}000 to 21{,}000 users at constant compute: $+4.3$, $+4.4$, $+4.7$, $+4.3\pt$. Every paired bootstrap interval contains zero. Meanwhile \textbf{22.4\pt} separate the four behavioural profiles and \textbf{42.0\pt} separate the record-count groups.},
  10/{19 Aug}/{\textbf{11 --- Why it climbs, then stops.} Three regimes on one curve: imitation below 500 users, generalisation from 500 to 2{,}000 ($+3.6\pt$), saturation above ($+0.1\pt$). One mechanism fits the timing: the smallest profile crosses $\approx100$ members exactly between the two tiers where the score moves.}}
