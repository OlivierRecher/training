\foreach [count=\j from 0] \date/\body in {%
  {3--8 Jul}/{\textbf{1. Read up, and get a first signal.} Which training method fits a free graphics card? Chose QLoRA over the two alternatives, and built the cleaning and formatting pipeline. \textbf{16.87\,\%} correct next-antenna answers on a Mistral-7B run that saw 1.2\,\% of the data.},
  {9--14 Jul}/{\textbf{2. The one-line rule appears, and the model is below it.} Abandoned Mistral-7B (5.5\,h, best 41.1\,\%) for Qwen2.5-0.5B. Fixes to the training machinery alone lifted the score \textbf{48.7\,\% $\rightarrow$ 64.4\,\%} and cut the waiting time by 60\,\%, but the one-line rule on that data sits at \textbf{69.2\,\%}.},
  {14--17 Jul}/{\textbf{3. Who is hard, and why.} Looked at the held-out people one at a time: the score tracks how often somebody changes antenna at \textbf{$r=-0.97$}, and 10\,\% of people carry 59\,\% of the graded moments. What the model adds lives in the segment that barely counts.},
  {17--21 Jul}/{\textbf{4. Filter the data, and win once.} Removed people with too few records or a four-hour hole in their day. \textbf{80.1\,\% against a 79.0\,\% rule: $+1.1$}, the first time the model cleared it, and only at one cut out of three.},
  {21--24 Jul}/{\textbf{5. Cleaner data, and the win evaporates.} Moved to the people who move and stay in the area, extracted by Nino. Swept the cut from 30\,\% to 90\,\%: \emph{no} cut beats the rule, the closest being 0.3 short. Looking person by person, some collapse to 0\,\% precisely at the end of the day.},
  {27--29 Jul}/{\textbf{6. Give the model the time of day.} Added one hour symbol per event and made mistakes cost more inside 04--06h and 18--20h. The hour \emph{is} read: shuffling it costs 3.0. Showing those slices more often then won the chosen hours (\textbf{$+2.6$} at 18--20h) and lost everywhere else ($-2.6$).},
  {29 Jul -- 2 Aug}/{\textbf{7. Sort people into kinds of user.} Four profiles cut on how often the \emph{mast} changes, hour by hour, and identified from the person's past alone (92\,\% agreement). The old global hours were shown to separate nothing (0.2) while the derived ones separate 27.4. First attempt at cross-training: \textbf{inconclusive}, and the checks showed the model ignoring both the kind of user and the hour.},
  {2--7 Aug}/{\textbf{8. Make the kind of user impossible to ignore.} Four changes; the structural one sends each kind of user to its own set of trained numbers instead of writing it into the text. \textbf{46.8\,\% against 43.1\,\%: $+3.7$}, the first run of the project to clear the rule. The gains rank inversely to difficulty, without exception.},
  {12--17 Aug}/{\textbf{9. More people.} 900 / 1{,}400 / 1{,}900 training people on a new day file: \textbf{$+2.8$, $+3.5$, $+4.2$}. Also tested stretching every day to 200 records: 74.9\,\% looks spectacular against a rule that reaches 76.5\,\% on the same data, and the result changes sign.},
  {18--19 Aug}/{\textbf{10. Many more people.} 5{,}000 to 21{,}000 people at constant computing time: $+4.3$, $+4.4$, $+4.7$, $+4.3$. Every resampling interval contains zero. Meanwhile \textbf{22.4} separate the four kinds of user, against 0.4 across the whole ladder.},
  {19 Aug}/{\textbf{11. Why it climbs, then stops.} Three regions on one curve: imitation at 400 people, generalisation from 400 to 1{,}900 ($+3.6$), saturation above ($+0.1$). One mechanism fits the timing: the smallest kind of user crosses about a hundred members exactly between the two sizes where the score moves.}}
